African St Laurent, Warped

One authored marble slab, shown four ways — pristine, subtly wrong, screaming, and turned inside out.

The fiction it was authored to: stone cut from a volume of space the warp got into. The scorch at the central junction is where something older held that zone — a wound that is also a protection. Ordinary eyes see the burned slab in daylight. A psyker sees what is under it. Same stone, same geometry, two sights.

composited  Both of those come off the same 65,535 × 40,959 master — 2.68 gigapixels, rendered as a grid of tiles straight to a tiled BigTIFF and downsampled here to 1600 px. The burn is painted on after the render: it is the one thing on this page the slab does not contain. Everything else — every vein, speck, fog bank and the entire spectrum change — is the renderer. The unburned masters are at the bottom of this page.

Stone Author draws stone as vector geometry, not pixels: veins, specks, fog and warp are a few thousand simple shapes driven by a small recipe. The recipe — a .json slab — is the master, and the render is a deterministic function of it: within one browser build the same slab renders byte-identical every time — the gallery harness gates exactly that, twice from a fresh load — and the same geometry can be drawn at any size. Across browser builds the picture is the same and the bytes are not, because sub-pixel anti-aliasing moves; that comparison is recorded rather than gated. The four steps below came off two slabs, rendered at 1600 × 1000; the post-steps that three of them add are recorded in reference.json, parameter by parameter.

Daylight and psyker are the same slab. One authored geometry, two lights: at rest it's near-true; under the other spectrum the full distortion it was always carrying is exposed.

Then turn the mask inside out. The same disc, in the same place, asked the opposite question: not what did the warp fail to reach but what is the only thing it reached.

The masters, unmarked

pure render  The same two sights with nothing added — no burn, no compositing, no retouch. These are the 2.68-gigapixel masters downsampled to 1600 px, and they are what the slab actually produces. Each master carries its own record of the render that made it — build, engine, hardware class, tile plan, timings, and a device identity sealed under the slab's own bytes — readable with node tools/tiff-meta.mjs <file>, which is how every figure published about this renderer is checked. Both were rendered on a 12-core desktop under Blink, 65,535 × 40,959 in 40 passes of 8192.
