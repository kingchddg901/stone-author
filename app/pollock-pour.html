How it pours. Paint leaves at a steady rate in time, so where your hand slows it pools and where it hurries it thins; hold still and the puddle keeps spreading. On a fast, tight bend, drops fly off along the tangent and land outside the curve, and lifting while you are still moving flicks a tail. Moving a slider re-pours every line from the same gesture.

Mercury bands is the simplest case: slow, straight, wide pours along mercury-light's grain (−72°), with the same specks as the ground, only denser. The real stone's speck density varies 1.7× more than random across its grain and not at all along it.

The specks are grown like the measured noise granites: sizes spread lognormally, about half are elongated, and the long ones loosely follow the stroke.
